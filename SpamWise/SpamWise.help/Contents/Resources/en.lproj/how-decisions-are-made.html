How decisions are made

SpamWise checks each message against the following, in order. The first one that applies decides the message.

  1. Sender rules. A rule for the sender’s email address wins over a rule for its domain. A Block rule always applies. An Allow rule applies only when the account has a trusted mail server that confirms the message came from the sender’s domain. Otherwise it only makes Junk less likely.
  2. Content rules. Rules that match a message’s subject, body or headers. When several match, the newest one decides. Like sender rules, a content Block rule always applies, and a content Allow rule decides only mail a trusted mail server confirms; otherwise it only makes Junk less likely. See Content rules.
  3. People you know. Senders in Contacts, people you’ve written to, and stores that sent you order confirmations go to Inbox. Like Allow rules, this requires a trusted mail server that confirms the sender; otherwise it only makes Junk less likely.
  4. Campaigns you marked as Junk. When Recognize campaigns you marked as Junk is on in Settings → Advanced, a message that closely matches one is marked Review.
  5. Your provider. Everything else goes to the provider you chose in Settings → Provider, with your mail choices, your additional instructions and some of your similar corrections. It answers Good, Review or Junk, says what kind of mail the message is, such as a store promotion or a newsletter, and gives a confidence. Your choice for that kind of mail then applies: Keep never moves it to Junk, Flag always marks it Review, and Junk moves it once the answer is confident enough. A Junk answer below the confidence your filtering strength requires becomes Review.

If part of a message can’t be read reliably, a Good answer becomes Review, so you can look at it yourself.

Trust an account’s mail server

A sender’s address can be forged. So Allow rules and people you know send mail straight to Inbox only when SpamWise trusts the server that receives the account’s mail and that server confirms the sender. In Settings → Accounts, each account shows whether Recognize senders you know is on, and which server it trusts.

  • Once SpamWise has detected the account’s server from new mail, it shows it as the Detected server. Choose Trust Detected Server.
  • Or choose Enter Server… (or Enter Another…) and enter the server yourself. Experts can find it named first in the Authentication-Results header of the account’s mail.

To change the server, choose Edit…. To go back, choose Stop Trusting…, then Stop Trusting: mail from people you know and senders with Allow rules then only becomes less likely to be Junk. Block rules don’t need a trusted server.

Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com accounts

Microsoft 365, Outlook.com and some similar providers name their server only in a header called ARC-Authentication-Results. SpamWise detects the server there too and shows it as the Detected server, with a note that it came from ARC headers. You can also choose Enter Server… and enter mx.microsoft.com yourself.

SpamWise reads only the ARC header the provider adds when mail arrives, never one a sender includes, and only to learn which server received the message. Whether the sender is confirmed still comes from the provider’s own Authentication-Results header. Mail that reached the account through another service that adds its own ARC headers, such as a forwarding service or a mailing list, isn’t confirmed this way, so Allow rules and people you know only make it less likely to be Junk.

Your addresses and exceptions

Spam often pretends to come from your own address, so SpamWise doesn’t count your own addresses as people you know. This is the Don’t treat my own addresses as people I know setting in Settings → Accounts, under Contacts and history, which is on unless you turn it off. SpamWise finds your addresses when you choose Check Mail Connection, or Check Connection for an IMAP account whose username is an email address, and lists them under Your addresses and exceptions.

Under Your addresses and exceptions, you can also enter other addresses, one per line, whose mail never counts as from people you know, such as an address of yours SpamWise didn’t find. That mail isn’t blocked, and your sender rules still apply.

Choose what you want to receive

Graymail is legitimate bulk mail whose value depends on you: store promotions, cart reminders, newsletters, product news, event offers, social network notifications, surveys, card and loan offers, fundraising, sales pitches and mail from clubs and groups. In Settings → Mail Choices, choose Keep, Flag or Junk for each kind. Personal mail, receipts and account notices always stay in your Inbox. At first, promotions, cart reminders, card and loan offers, fundraising and sales pitches are flagged, and the rest are kept.

Under Additional instructions, add anything the choices don’t cover in your own words, such as “Newsletters about aviation are fine.” Your provider reads them with the messages it classifies, and they win where they are more specific. Changes are saved as you make them. To restore the first choices and clear your instructions, choose Restore Defaults…, then Restore Defaults.

Your corrections suggest changes too. When you correct several messages of one kind from different senders the same way, Mail Choices offers that choice for the whole kind under Suggested from your corrections. Nothing changes until you choose it.

Learn from your Junk folders

Your Junk folders already show what you don’t want. During setup, or in Settings → Mail Choices, choose Scan Junk Folders. SpamWise reads up to 400 recent Junk messages per account and lists their senders. Senders in your Contacts or mail history are left out unless you include them under Choose senders. Choose Save Examples to keep one example from each chosen sender as a reviewed Junk example. This creates no rules and moves no mail; manage the examples under Reviewed messages and learning. The examples keep no message text unless Keep text of reviewed messages is on. Scanning works with Apple Mail.

What SpamWise doesn’t hold against a message

Missing permissions, failed lookups and unknown history never count as signs of spam. SpamWise never loads remote images or opens links in messages. When a message can’t be classified, for example because the provider is unavailable, it stays where it is and Activity shows it as Unclassified.
