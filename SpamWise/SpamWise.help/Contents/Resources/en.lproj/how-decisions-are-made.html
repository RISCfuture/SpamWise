How decisions are made

SpamWise checks each message against the following, in order. The first one that applies decides the message.

  1. Sender rules. A rule for the sender’s email address wins over a rule for its domain. A Block rule always applies. An Allow rule applies only when the account has a trusted mail server that confirms the message came from the sender’s domain. Otherwise it only makes Junk less likely.
  2. Content rules. Rules that match a message’s subject, body or headers. When several match, the newest one decides. See Content rules.
  3. People you know. Senders in Contacts, people you’ve written to, and stores that sent you order confirmations go to Inbox. Like Allow rules, this requires a trusted mail server that confirms the sender; otherwise it only makes Junk less likely.
  4. Campaigns you marked as Junk. When Recognize campaigns you marked as Junk is on in Settings → Advanced, a message that closely matches one is marked Review.
  5. Your provider. Everything else goes to the provider you chose in Settings → Classifier, with your preferences and a few of your similar corrections. It answers Good, Review or Junk, says what kind of mail the message is, such as a store promotion or a newsletter, and gives a confidence. Your choice for that kind of mail then applies: Keep never moves it to Junk, Flag always marks it Review, and Junk moves it once the answer is confident enough. A Junk answer below the confidence your filtering strength requires becomes Review.

If part of a message can’t be read reliably, a Good answer becomes Review, so you can look at it yourself.

Trust an account’s mail server

A sender’s address can be forged. So Allow rules and people you know send mail straight to Inbox only when SpamWise trusts the server that receives the account’s mail and that server confirms the sender. In Settings → Accounts, each account shows its Trusted mail server.

  • Once SpamWise has detected the account’s server from new mail, choose Trust Detected Server.
  • Or choose Enter Server… and enter the server named first in the account’s Authentication-Results headers.

Choose Stop Trusting to go back. Block rules and content rules don’t need a trusted server.

Choose what you want to receive

Graymail is legitimate bulk mail whose value depends on you: store promotions, cart reminders, newsletters, product news, event offers, social network notifications, surveys, card and loan offers, fundraising, sales pitches and mail from clubs and groups. In Settings → Preferences, choose Keep, Flag or Junk for each kind. Personal mail, receipts and account notices always stay in your Inbox. At first, promotions, cart reminders, card and loan offers, fundraising and sales pitches are flagged, and the rest are kept.

Under Additional instructions, add anything the choices don’t cover in your own words, such as “Newsletters about aviation are fine.” Your provider reads them with every message it classifies, and they win where they are more specific. Changes are saved as you make them. Choose Reset to defaults to restore the first choices and clear your instructions.

Your corrections suggest changes too. When you correct several messages of one kind from different senders the same way, Settings → Preferences offers to apply that choice to the whole kind. Nothing changes until you choose it.

Learn from your Junk folders

Your Junk folders already show what you don’t want. During setup, or in Settings → Preferences, choose Scan Junk Folders. SpamWise reads up to 400 recent Junk messages per account and lists their senders. Senders in your Contacts or mail history are left out unless you include them. Choose Save Examples to keep one example from each chosen sender as a reviewed Junk example. This creates no rules and moves no mail; manage the examples under Reviewed messages and learning. Scanning works with Apple Mail.

What SpamWise doesn’t hold against a message

Missing permissions, failed lookups and unknown history never count as signs of spam. SpamWise never loads remote images or opens links in messages. When a message can’t be classified, for example because the provider is unavailable, it stays where it is and Activity shows it as Unclassified.
